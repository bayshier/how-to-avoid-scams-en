# How to Avoid Scams

**Scams change costumes. The manipulation never does.** Five moves — fear, authority, greed, urgency, isolation — sit behind every con, from "pig-butchering" romance-investment fraud to fake police on a video call.

> 🚨 **Being pressured to transfer money, install an app, or share your screen right now?** Stop reading and hang up. Then call your bank and the police. *(In China: 110, or answer 96110. Elsewhere: your local police, or a national reporting line such as [IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov) in the US or Action Fraud in the UK.)* This guide is for before it happens — not for putting out a fire.

## Three rules that stop most scams

1. **Never click unfamiliar links; never share verification codes.** The one-time code is the last lock on your account. No real support agent will ask for it — or ask you to install remote-control software.
2. **Any job that asks you to pay first is a scam.** Deposits, training fees, "unfreezing fees" — the more names for the fee, the faster you should walk.
3. **Hang up, then call back on a number you looked up yourself.** Caller ID and video calls can both be faked. The number *they* gave you proves nothing.

## What this book is


Not a novel, not a textbook — a **field manual of how scams actually work**:

- **One scam = one story.** 405 teardowns in the original; each opens with one line on what the scam is after.
- **Every story follows the same skeleton.** The script in numbered steps (1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣), where the trap is (⚠️), how to defend (✅), which law applies (⚖️), and a one-line wake-up call (🛑).
- **Three tags per story.** ① Scam category (16 categories) ② What you lose (money? privacy? criminal liability?) ③ Who it targets (the elderly? students? merchants?).
- **Every story links to real cases.** The original carries 1,601 references to public notices from courts, procuratorates, police and state media — same scam type, real cases.

In other words: **you don't read it cover to cover. Something happens, you search the keyword.**

## How to read it

- **Don't read cover to cover.** Something happened? Search the keyword — a platform, a phrase the caller used, the "fee" they want.
- **Quote the script.** Every story includes the actual lines scammers say. If a call matches one, you have your answer.
- **Share the Five Blades essay** with the person who needs it — a parent, a teenager, a colleague who "met someone online".

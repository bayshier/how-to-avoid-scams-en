# Glossary · Scam Slang Quick Reference

## Scam Types

**"Pig-butchering" (杀猪盘)**　<sub>Common in: romance · adult-entrapment scams / investment · crypto</sub>

The umbrella term for romance-pivoting-to-investment scams. The scammer first "fattens the pig" — building a romance or a close-confidant relationship through chat — then "butchers" it: luring the victim into depositing and investing on a fake platform, then vanishing with the money. Most romance-category and investment-category scams in this book are variants of it.

The shadow trade in stolen 'romance leads'," #231 "The truth about 'pig-butchering': every sudden 'destiny' is nothing but careful design"</sub>

**"Order-brushing" (刷单)**　<sub>Common in: part-time gigs · order-brushing</sub>

Faking online purchases to inflate a shop's sales figures, supposedly for commission. The first few small orders pay out to build trust; then "big orders" and "chained orders" require you to front the money — and once fronted, it never comes back. Order-brushing is itself illegal, and neither platforms nor police will ever assign you "advance-payment tasks."

<sub>Related teardowns: #147 "Exposed! From first sweet taste to chained traps: wiped out in a single order-brushing scam," #34 "Campus survey questionnaires as arbitrage tools? The 'survey-brushing' gray-black industry," #322 "'Crab gift cards' and redemption vouchers in mystery packages: the new face of order-brushing scams"</sub>

**"Pay first" / fronting your own money (垫付)**　<sub>Common in: part-time gigs · order-brushing / daily life · bills & tickets</sub>

The pivotal action word inside scams: you pay out of your own pocket first, with a promise of repayment plus commission afterwards. Whenever "pay first" appears in a part-time gig, an insurance claim, or a refund process, the net is already closing.

**"Brick-moving" arbitrage (搬砖)**　<sub>Common in: investment · crypto</sub>

Ponzi-style schemes branded as "buy low, sell high, pocket the spread": crypto brick-moving, brick-moving arbitrage desks… The returns come entirely from new members' money paying old members' interest, and collapse is only a matter of time.

<sub>Related teardowns: #238 "Inside the 'game brick-moving' scam: 'easy 1,000 yuan a day'? Don't be naive — you're only the next…," #105 "'10,000 yuan a month AFK gaming'? **Lead-the-trade / call-the-trade (带单/喊单)**　<sub>Common in: investment · crypto / online gambling</sub>

A "teacher" in a livestream or a group directs your trades: first they deliberately let you win two small rounds, then push you into bigger deposits or steer you onto a fake trading desk. The teacher can see your positions; you can't see their back end.

**The fake "desk" (盘口)**　<sub>Common in: online gambling / investment · crypto</sub>

A fake trading platform where you bet on "up or down." The front end looks like live international market data; every number on the back end is edited by the scammer — what you win is a figure on a screen, what you lose is real money, and withdrawals are never approved.

**Martingale betting (倍投)**　<sub>Common in: online gambling</sub>

The gambler's algorithm: "double your bet after every loss and you'll win it all back." Mathematically, a few consecutive losses make the required bankroll grow exponentially — no wallet survives it. The only guaranteed winner in any gamble is the house.

<sub>Related teardowns: #290 "World Cup anti-fraud: the betting money you lost — whose pocket did it actually land in?"</sub>

**Score up / score down (上分/下分)**　<sub>Common in: online gambling</sub>

Gambling-platform slang: depositing is "scoring up," withdrawing is "scoring down." On a scammer-controlled platform, score-ups land instantly; score-downs run into "taxes," "unfreezing fees," and "insufficient turnover" — and never complete.

**Recruiting heads (拉人头)**　<sub>Common in: the dark-industry big picture / job hunting · franchising</sub>

The core move of pyramid schemes: income comes not from selling anything but from recruiting downlines. Three-tier distribution, per-head commissions, steep franchise fees — all of it musical chairs; under Chinese law, three tiers are enough to constitute the pyramid-scheme crime.

**"Cutting leeks" (割韭菜)**　<sub>Common in: investment · crypto / romance · adult-entrapment scams</sub>

The metaphor for harvesting victims over and over, crop after crop. Livestream "fam" and stock-tip-group "students" are the leeks standing in the field; when the house dumps its holdings, it runs faster than anyone.

**"Guzi" — anime merchandise (谷子)**　<sub>Common in: elders · students · moms</sub>

Fandom slang (borrowed from "goods") for anime and game merchandise: badges, standees, trading cards. The scams around "eating guzi" — cut-rate proxy buying, group-buy runaways, "free card" intimidation schemes — target minors, often with fake police frightening children into transferring money from a parent's phone.

<sub>Related teardowns: #337 "'Guzi' merch, trading cards, free game skins: a new scam combo targeting school kids"</sub>

**Trap loans (套路贷)**　<sub>Common in: online loans · trap loans</sub>

Lending in name, seizure in substance: upfront deductions, artificially inflated debts, loan-churning to "roll over" balances, malicious stacking of the sum owed — ending in violent collection or sham lawsuits that force you to hand over your house or your car. Remember: legitimate institutions do not cold-offer loans to "people desperate for cash."

**"Beheaded" interest — the upfront deduction (砍头息)**　<sub>Common in: online loans · trap loans</sub>

Borrow 10,000, receive 7,000, pay interest on the full 10,000. The cut-off 3,000 is the "beheaded" interest — it pushes the real annualized rate past 100% with ease, and it is the basic lever trap loans use to stack your debt.

**Slow top-up (慢充)**　<sub>Common in: online shopping · secondhand parcels / daily life · bills & tickets</sub>

Phone-plan or membership top-ups offered far below face value, credited slowly. The funding source is most likely laundered dirty money: your "discount recharge" gives the money laundry one more pass-through hop, and the number you topped up may itself get dragged into a criminal case.

## Supply-Chain Roles

**Money muling (跑分)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

Moving criminal proceeds through your own bank card or payment QR code for a percentage of the flow. Every transfer happens under your name — when the police follow the money chain backward, the first name they reach is yours, on suspicion of the helper offense or the concealment offense.

<sub>Related teardowns: #135 "Exposed! Beware! So-called 'crypto money muling' is just the dark industry harvesting…," #25 "Teardown of the 'no-deposit muling' instant dog-eat-dog scam: 'zero deposit, paid first,' they claim," #369 "Tens of thousands per order? The most complete teardown of 'go abroad and get rich' traps"</sub>

**The "money laundry" (水房)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards / the dark-industry big picture</sub>

The crew whose specialty is "washing" dirty money: splitting it into small amounts, across many cards, through many layers of transfers, and finally cashing out through cryptocurrency or gift cards. Money mules are the laundry's outermost pair of hands.

<sub>Related teardowns: #138 "Exposed! Why does my withdrawal of a few thousand get me interrogated while scammers move millions unhindered?"</sub>

**Card farmers (卡农)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

The people who acquire and stockpile bank cards to sell to the money laundry. One "four-piece set" sells for a few hundred to over a thousand yuan — and from then on, the seller answers for every yuan that ever moves through that card.

<sub>Related teardowns: #185 "The dark industry's internal 'cant,' fully exposed! Learn to hear it — and stay off the path," #328 "The seven anti-fraud tools the state gives you — most people have never used a single one"</sub>

**Dog promoters (狗推)**　<sub>Common in: the dark-industry big picture / job hunting · franchising</sub>

The lowest-tier salespeople inside overseas scam compounds, posing as "tall, rich, and kind" dream partners, "customer service," or "teachers" on social apps to cast wide nets. The pay is low and missed quotas bring beatings — and many of them were themselves lured there by "high-paying job offers."

**The four-piece set (四件套)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

Bank card + ID card + SIM card + USB security key (U盾, or a phone-banking binding) — the standard toolkit for mule-based laundering. Selling or renting out your four-piece set means selling the criminal liability along with it.

**Lead generation (引流)**　<sub>Common in: part-time gigs · order-brushing / the dark-industry big picture</sub>

The first pass of the funnel, moving people from public platforms into private traps: ads planted in short-video comment sections, dating apps, and gig-hunting groups; add on WeChat, herd into groups, then hand the targets to the scammers "downstream" for processing. A large share of "liker" and "assistant" part-time gigs are actually this.

**Scripts (话术)**　<sub>Common in: impersonation · fake police/prosecutors/courts / the dark-industry big picture</sub>

The scripts scammers read from: every call and every message follows a standard flow — how to open, how to answer doubts, how to push for the transfer. The person on the phone is reading a script; behind the script sits a psychologist.

**Whitewashing (洗白)**　<sub>Common in: online loans · trap loans / money laundering · bank cards</sub>

Two meanings, both traps. One: laundering dirty money (the money laundry's job). Two: "credit-record whitewashing" — claims that paying someone can erase bad entries from your credit file. Pure fraud: overdue records clear automatically five years after repayment.

**Account aging (养号)**　<sub>Common in: AI · account theft / privacy · the information black market</sub>

Nursing freshly registered social or bank accounts with ordinary-looking usage for a while, so they slip past platform risk controls before being used in crimes. On the market, an "aged account" costs far more than a fresh one.

**Card dealers (卡商)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

Middlemen who open or buy up cards in bulk and resell them to the dark industry. At the issuing link they are the fountainhead of "two-card" crime (bank cards and phone cards), connecting downward to the street and upward to the money laundry.

**Phone-relay rigs ("shoujikou," 手机口)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards / job hunting · franchising</sub>

A makeshift call-forwarding rig built from two phones and one audio cable: one phone connects the scammer abroad, the other dials the victim in-country — so the overseas call displays a local number. The "a few hundred yuan an hour" phone-relay gig is the helper offense; multiple students have already been sentenced over it.

**Cash-out runners (取现车手)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

People who withdraw cash offline for fraud gangs, or collect cash and gold and relay it layer by layer, using ride-hailing cars and vans for the handoffs. Knowing the money is fraud-linked and still helping move it is the concealment offense — the final stop after "daily pay of a few hundred" is handcuffs.

**Adult-traffic "fans" (色粉)**　<sub>Common in: romance · adult-entrapment scams</sub>

## Legal Terms

**Card cut-off (断卡)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards / emergency · anti-fraud tools</sub>

The "Card Cut-Off Campaign": China's nationwide special crackdown on fraud-linked bank cards and phone cards, running since 2020. Anyone on the sanctions list cannot open new cards for 5 years and faces restricted non-counter banking — the price of renting out a card goes far beyond one lawsuit.

<sub>Related teardowns: #20 "Stop assuming telecom fraud only targets Chinese people! The truth about Southeast Asian compounds: hunting victims worldwide"</sub>

**The helper offense (帮信罪)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards / job hunting · franchising</sub>

Full name: the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities (Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II). Knowingly helping others commit crimes over information networks — selling cards, money muling, lead generation, rigging up GOIP devices — is enough to constitute it. Students and job seekers are the hardest-hit group, and the starting line is already a criminal record.

<sub>Related teardowns: #341 "'Two phones, one cable, 600 yuan a day': the high-paying 'phone relay' gig," #14 "Free tissues for a QR scan? Your account may become the fraud gang's 'lead-gen tool'," #49 "Don't let fat commissions go to your head! How the 'edge-ball lead generation' gray industry reels you in"</sub>

**The concealment offense (掩隐罪)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

Full name: the crime of concealing or disguising criminal proceeds. Heavier than the helper offense: money muling that reaches a certain flow volume, or transferring funds you know are dirty, is handled under this offense — up to seven years.

<sub>Related teardowns: #166 "Thought you'd snagged a bargain — turns out you were laundering money for the dark industry," #39 "Thought 'six layers of separation' made you invisible? Inside 'gift card / fuel card'…," #85 "Inside the hidden fat-margin trade in Apple top-up cards: monthly flows in the millions"</sub>

**Card cut-off sanctions (断卡惩戒)**　<sub>Common in: money laundering · bank cards</sub>

The credit sanctions imposed once renting or selling bank cards is confirmed: 5 years without non-counter banking services, no new cards, restricted payment accounts. Your financial life is cut in half — a price far beyond that few-hundred-yuan "rent."

## Tools

**GOIP**　<sub>Common in: impersonation · fake police/prosecutors/courts / the dark-industry big picture</sub>

A bulk-call device that turns one SIM into "a pile of numbers": scammers abroad remotely control hardware inside the country to place calls, and the caller ID shows a local number. So "the number is local" has never meant "the person is local."

<sub>Related teardowns: #341 "'Two phones, one cable, 600 yuan a day': the high-paying 'phone relay' gig," #87 "Inside the 'field photography and device inspection' gig scam: '90 yuan a shot,' they claim"</sub>

**SIM banks ("cat pools," 猫池)**　<sub>Common in: privacy · the information black market / the dark-industry big picture</sub>

Devices that hold dozens or hundreds of SIM cards and send, receive, and place texts and calls in bulk — used to harvest verification codes, age accounts, and blast scam SMS.

<sub>Related teardowns: #211 "Your idle SIM card is earning someone tens of thousands a night? Inside the SIM…," #56 "100,000 texts a day: the million-yuan dream stacked out of SIM-bank devices," #18 "Caught red-handed! Over a hundred yuan for opening a card? Carrier 'insider' collusion"</sub>

**Code-receiving services (接码)**　<sub>Common in: privacy · the information black market / emergency · anti-fraud tools</sub>

Verification-code reception as a service: the dark industry combines "code platforms" with SIM banks holding thousands of cards to register accounts in bulk. You don't even know what your phone number has been used to register — "One-ID Lookup" will tell you.

**Phishing links (钓鱼链接)**　<sub>Common in: impersonation · fake police/prosecutors/courts / daily life · bills & tickets</sub>

Fake pages imitating official websites, built to harvest your account, password, and verification codes. 99% of the links in text messages are not the service they claim to be — type the official address yourself; never tap the one inside the message.

**Screen sharing (屏幕共享)**　<sub>Common in: impersonation · fake police/prosecutors/courts / daily life · bills & tickets</sub>

Live-sharing your phone screen with the other party. Once it's on, your banking password, SMS codes, and account balances are all broadcast — the moment "customer service" asks you to start a screen share "for assistance," the net has begun closing.

**96110**　<sub>Common in: emergency · anti-fraud tools</sub>

China's national anti-fraud hotline. When it calls you, it's a fraud warning and intervention (always pick up); when you call it, it's consultation and reporting. The golden half-hour after being scammed: dial 96110/110 first to freeze payments, then go to the police station.

**One-ID Lookup (一证通查)**　<sub>Common in: emergency · anti-fraud tools / privacy · the information black market</sub>

An official service run by China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology: with one ID number, look up every phone card and internet account registered under your name. Any card you didn't register yourself: cancel it immediately — it may be working for the scammers right now.

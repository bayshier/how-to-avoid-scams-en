## #196 · "Zero experience, guaranteed job, ¥10k-plus a month"? The 8 major job-hunting traps and black-industry plays on mainstream recruiting platforms

`Jobs · Franchising` `🚫Account bans` `🔓Privacy leak` `🎯Students` `🎯Job seekers` `🎯Debtors`

For many fresh graduates and job hunters, the first pit they step into is a scam on a mainstream recruiting platform.
Scammers exploit platform rule loopholes (for example, buying other people's business licenses to run a fake front), and post large volumes of fake high-pay jobs. Here is the full inventory of the black-industry plays rampaging across recruiting platforms — don't step on them. 💸

**🕵️ Deep breakdown: the 8 typical job-hunting plays on recruiting platforms**

1️⃣ Type 1: Recruit-to-train and training-loan traps (employment that becomes debt) 📈
Typical posts: Java/Python developer, UI design, short-video editing, film post-production, hands-on accounting, HR.
The play: billed as "zero experience welcome, guaranteed placement, ¥8k–15k/month." After the interview, you are forced into paid training before "placement," and steered into taking out training loans of tens of thousands of yuan — then fobbed off with a low-pay outsourcing gig, or simply dismissed.
2️⃣ Type 2: Online part-time work — a "pig-butchering" variant (romance-investment scams that take both money and feelings) 🪤
Typical posts: AI data-labeler, short-video/image reviewer, online data-entry clerk.
The play: "hundreds a day without leaving home." Small rebates build trust; then you are guided into large advance-funded "tasks" you can never withdraw from — some victims are even maneuvered into sexual blackmail via explicit video calls.
3️⃣ Type 3: Advance-fee fraud (the classic fee grab) 💰
Typical posts: machine operators, warehouse sorters, security guards, receptionists, caregivers — clerical and blue-collar roles.
The play: low entry bar, decent-looking pay. After you join, they demand private transfers of hundreds to thousands of yuan for "uniform fees, badge fees, medical-check fees, deposits" — then dismiss you for "failing the assessment."
4️⃣ Type 4: Overpriced vehicle rentals and purchases (fake drivers and riders) 🚗
Typical posts: delivery riders, ride-hail drivers, freight drivers.
The play: pay far above market average is promised, but you must rent a vehicle at inflated prices or finance one through them. After you start, dispatch is deliberately starved so your daily income can't cover the rent — pushing you into "breach" and heavy penalty fees.
5️⃣ Type 5: "Crowd-filling" gigs and fraud-app card binding (becoming a tool person) 📲
Typical posts: crowd-filling part-time work (paid just to show up, fill a room, or register for apps), app testers.
The play: "a few relaxed hours for a few hundred yuan" — but you must download designated third-party apps and bind your bank card. Many of these apps are fraud- or gambling-linked illegal software; your bank card can even get frozen as a result.
6️⃣ Type 6: Résumé reselling and privacy theft (data trafficking) 📝
The play: using bought credentials, scammers post masses of fake high-pay jobs, harvest résumés in bulk — even demanding ID numbers and bank-card details — then package and sell the whole lot.
7️⃣ Type 7: Remote and overseas high-pay traps (risk to life and limb) ✈️
The play: "work abroad," "high pay on the border" — in the vast majority of cases these are MLM organizations or telecom-fraud rings recruiting; people can be lured out of the country into mortal danger.
8️⃣ Type 8: Borderline "services" and sexual extortion (the shady high-pay lane) 🔞
Typical posts: billiards hostess, bar promoter, "private assistant," paid companion.
The play: "tens of thousands a month," demands for height and body measurements; offline "interviews" may trick you into private photos later used for blackmail.

> 🛡️ Anti-scam reminders and a job-hunting pitfall guide:
> 🚫 Never transfer money or pay deposits privately: any job that demands uniform fees, training fees, or deposits before you start is 100% a setup!
> 🔒 Keep communication on the platform: do all communicating and contract-signing on the legitimate platform or official channels; never move to private chat and transfer money.
> 🚨 Refuse loans and overpriced rentals: you are job hunting to make money — any position that requires "take a loan first / rent equipment first" should be reported outright.
> 🏷️ Treat abnormal pay rationally: a "low-barrier, extremely high-pay" offer that defies the market always has poison on the hook.
🛑 See through the job-hunting plays, dodge the high-pay traps, and guard both your money and your safety.

## #171 · Beware the "quick-franchise" trap wearing an entrepreneurship costume

`Jobs · Franchising` `🎯Debtors`

A deep breakdown of quick-franchise scams, to keep would-be entrepreneurs from being blinded by false promises of "low barriers, high returns, fast payback" and ending up broke and in debt.
Burning to start your own business and escape the salaried life, you can fall — quite casually — into a carefully engineered "quick-franchise" trap.
In recent years, so-called quick-franchise companies have run mass false advertising across online platforms, harvesting entrepreneurs' hard-earned money under the banner of investment franchising.
Today we lay out, once and for all, the core plays behind franchise scams and how to guard against them. 💸

**🕵️ Deep breakdown: the 3 core plays quick-franchise companies use to harvest entrepreneurs**

1️⃣ Knock off big brands, lure with false advertising 📈
They buy heavy ad placements on search engines and short-video platforms, deliberately mimicking the names of famous popular brands, or posing as their "sub-brand / second location," borrowing big-brand fame to capture aspiring franchisees' contact details and hook them.
2️⃣ Hire queuers, manufacture a boom 🪤
During the site-visit stage, quick-franchise companies hire actors to queue up, invent industry honors and awards, and verbally promise "each store earns fistfuls daily, payback in 3 months," staging a phony prosperity that pressures you to sign and pay on the spot.
3️⃣ Change face after payment; perform in bad faith 💰
Once the entrepreneur has paid a franchise fee of tens to hundreds of thousands of yuan, the promised site-selection guidance, operational support, and technical training evaporate. They even force you to buy shoddy equipment and supplies at far above market prices — squeezing the entrepreneur a second time.

> ⚠️ The brutal truth behind quick-franchise scams
> ❌ Harvest and run, then rebrand and harvest again: quick-franchise companies never intend to build a brand. The model is "recruit fast, harvest fast, deregister fast." The moment one brand's name goes rotten, they swap the shell and defraud the next batch.
> 💳 Justice is hard; the contracts are minefields: their franchise contracts are drafted by professional legal teams to shed legal risk, dressing "franchise fees" up as "consulting service fees" — making it very hard for defrauded entrepreneurs to sue the money back.

> 🛡️ Prevention guide and pitfall-avoidance advice
- 1️⃣ Verify corporate credentials and filings: a genuinely compliant franchise brand must hold a commercial franchise record filing (China's "two stores, one year" requirement), checkable in the Ministry of Commerce's franchise information system. No filing on record — be extremely wary!
- 2️⃣ Refuse verbal promises; do covert fieldwork: never rely solely on the "model stores" headquarters arranges. Pick several franchise outlets on your own — not the officially designated ones — and stake them out at different hours to observe real foot traffic and profitability.
- 3️⃣ Stay rational; refuse impulse swipes: faced with pressure lines like "pay today and get ¥20,000 off," do not get carried away. Entrepreneurship is hard — guard your hard-earned money! 💡

🛑 The internet is full of routines and scammers never run out. Stay alert, and hold your ground.

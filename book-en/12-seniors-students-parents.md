## #203 · A few yuan of cost, sold for thousands? The precision-harvest playbook behind "free gifts in health-wellness groups" aimed at the elderly

`Seniors · Students · Moms` `💸Money loss` `🎯Middle-aged & elderly`

"Free glucose-lowering patches, cash just for attending class every day, towels and eggs for signing in?"
Under the banner of "health, wellness, and pure public welfare," one operation siphoned more than ¥1.5 million in pension savings from over ten thousand seniors in just two months.
All that meticulous "care" is, in substance, a psychological harvest custom-built for elderly people who scrimped all their lives and now fear illness. 🚨

**🕵️ Deep breakdown: the four-step harvest aimed at the elderly in "wellness groups"**

1️⃣ Step 1: Free bait to filter targets (luring into groups) 🎁
Ad traffic: ads are placed on short-video platforms, baited with "free glucose-lowering patches," "cash red packets for attending classes," and "gifts for consecutive sign-ins."
Precision targeting: designed to draw in seniors with hypertension, high blood sugar and other chronic conditions — people who like small freebies and have weaker scam defenses — into so-called private "wellness groups."
2️⃣ Step 2: Register their details, infiltrate with "care" (building trust) 📝
Harvesting privacy: under the pretext of mailing the gifts, they register each senior's name, phone number, and home address.
Endlessly attentive: the "health teachers" ask after them every day — wear more when it's cold, take an umbrella when it rains — more caring than their own children.
Mapping the household: the chats are mined for pension size, whether they live alone, whether they habitually buy health supplements — all preparation for the precision harvest.
3️⃣ Step 3: Long-course brainwashing that filters buying power (numbing the will) 🧠
The first few days: five-minute mini-lessons with red packets worth a few cents.
The courses then stretch to a full hour, weeding out the impatient and filtering for targets who deeply trust the lecturer and have both the time and the money.
4️⃣ Step 4: Shoddy products sold "by treatment course" at huge markups (the profit harvest) 💰
After about 15 days of conditioning, out comes the "miracle drug" or supplement claimed to "cure all diseases / root out chronic illness."
The real cost: mostly white-label or "three-nos" products (no manufacturer, no certification, no production date) costing a few yuan.
The harvest price: several hundred to over a thousand yuan a box, with seniors urged to "buy a full treatment course" — a single harvest often exceeds ¥10,000.

> ⚠️ The essence revealed: you think you're grabbing freebies — he's aiming at your pension!
There is no free lunch. These rings exploit elderly people's longing for health, their loneliness and lack of companionship, and their thrift, packaging a few yuan of shoddy goods as a "life-saving miracle drug" sold at extortionate markups. 🛑
> 🛡️ Prevention reminders and pitfall guide:
> 🚫 Warn the elders at home about "free gifts": any health lecture that requires joining a group, signing in daily, and collecting gifts or red packets has, 100% of the time, a hard-sell trap waiting downstream.
> 🔒 Protect personal and family privacy: remind seniors never to reveal home address, pension income, or health conditions in WeChat groups.
> 🚨 For illness, go to a real hospital: supplements cannot replace medicine. When "special-effect drugs" are pushed at high prices, children should intervene promptly, keep the evidence, and report to the market regulator.
🛑 Give your parents more care and companionship — don't let heartless merchants exploit the "warmth trap." Forward this to the family group chat and keep your elders sharp. 👇

## #228 · Over ¥2 million in one month?! The fake-auction black-industry play built on "priceless old coins and grain coupons"

`Seniors · Students · Moms` `💸Money loss` `🎯Middle-aged & elderly`

"Old grain coupons (ration tickets from China's planned-economy era that many families still keep) and copper coins left from decades ago — a single one can sell for tens of thousands, even over a million?!"
If you or the elders in your family believe that, you are already close to being scammed. A junk website costs just ¥500 to build, and with "fake auctions" a black-industry ring can harvest over ¥2 million in a single month. Today we pull apart, piece by piece, the entire zero-cost "trade thin air for cash" logic.

**🔍 Step 1: Directed feeding, precisely catching "middle-aged and elderly targets" 🎯**

The rings blast ads across every major platform with irresistible copy: "If your family has old grain coupons, old coins, or old stamps, don't throw them away! The market is surging — one ticket can swap for an apartment!"
These ads are targeted specifically at the 50–60 age bracket. Elders who spent a lifetime being frugal find it very hard not to be tempted at the thought that idle "old objects" could turn into hundreds of thousands of yuan — and they message the sellers themselves.

> 💰 Step 2: Photo appraisal, sky-high quotes that set a "get-rich maze" 📈

The victim photographs the old coupons or coins and sends them over. Without ever seeing the actual item, the scammer quotes a heaven-high price within two minutes:
An ordinary grain coupon worth ¥5 — quoted at ¥280,000!
An ordinary copper coin worth ¥10 — appraised at ¥800,000–1.2 million!
To dispel doubt, the scammer floods the victim with forged transaction records, buyer-inquiry screenshots, and "past million-yuan auction successes," until the elder fully believes a fortune sits in their hands.

**📄 Step 3: Induced signing — the "contract trap" hiding behind verbal promises ⚠️**

The moment you want to cash out, the money demands begin: "To enter a high-end auction and meet serious collectors, you must pay service fees first."
Basic booth fee: ¥200–500
Premium promotion fee: ¥800–1,200
Rush / special-session fee: ¥1,500–2,100
The victim does the math — one or two thousand yuan for hundreds of thousands, what a bargain! The scammer even underwrites it verbally: "If it doesn't sell, full refund — we take not a cent if there's no sale!"
The truth: the fine print of the electronic contract they send states clearly that "once promotion services begin, fees are non-refundable whether or not the item sells." The verbal promises are theater; the contract clauses are the hidden kill shot.

**🗑️ Step 4: A self-made webpage pretends to list the item; after the "failed auction," they block and vanish 👻**

Once the money lands, there is no offline auction and there are no real buyers — the photos are simply uploaded to a self-built junk webpage nobody visits, left hanging for a few days.
When the deadline passes, the same reply goes to everyone: "Insufficient attention; bids did not reach the reserve price. The auction failed."
When the victim demands a refund, the scammer shoves the contract back at them; if the victim keeps pushing, they get blocked — and the scammer vanishes.

> 💸 How much can these scammers actually make?
Take a ring previously busted by police as an example:
Victims per month: 900+ 😵
Monthly fraud take: over ¥2 million 💵
Average loss: about ¥2,200 per person (elders with more collectibles were re-harvested for ¥5,000–6,000 in fees)
This play requires no inventory, no logistics, no cost — pure arbitrage on human weakness.
> 💡 Anti-scam guide: remember this one rule!

**🚨 A legitimate auction house charges commission only AFTER the sale!**

🛑 Any "auction house" that charges you upfront — promotion fees, booth fees, appraisal fees, listing fees — before your item has sold is 100% fraud. Forward this to the elders around you and guard their pension money! 🙅‍♂️✨

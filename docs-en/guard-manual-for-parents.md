# A Guard Manual for Parents & Elders: From Free Eggs to Retirement Projects — Taking the Whole Chain Apart

> Long-form essay · Synthesized from 11 teardowns in the "elders · students · moms" category plus related livelihood and investment teardowns. Written for adult children — with scams, one extra question from you can save your parents a hundred thousand yuan (about US$14,000).

Scams targeting the elderly don't appear one at a time. They come off **a complete production line**, with a dedicated specialist at every link. This essay takes the entire chain apart, then gives you a set of defensive moves you can finish explaining over one dinner.

## 1. The Production Line: How an Elder's Money Gets Carried Out the Door

```
Link 1  Harvest data: shipping labels, supplement sign-up sheets, free-health-check registrations → sold by the record on black markets
Link 2  Offer sweets: free eggs, free tours, free health checks → build the trust of "these people are good to me"
Link 3  Manufacture authority: white-coated "experts," caring livestream "hosts," "Teacher Sun" → make judgments on Mom and Dad's behalf
Link 4  Tell stories: supplements can replace medication, collectibles will double, eldercare beds with high-yield leasebacks → build a dream that holds together
Link 5  Collect the money: small trial payments → large "treatment courses" and "pre-IPO shares" → mortgaged houses, drained retirement savings
Link 6  Seal the exits: "Don't tell your kids, they wouldn't understand" → isolate the last person who could pull the brake
```

Check this chain against real cases. In the major livestream elder-fraud case in Zhongning announced by Xinhua, the livestream's "caring hosts" sold unlicensed supplements (so-called "three-nos" products: no manufacturer, no quality certification, no approval number); the case involved 10 million yuan (about US$1.4 million), most victims were elderly, and twelve people were arrested ([Xinhuanet](http://www.ningxia.xinhua.org/20260713/b716d1a7166444119d04cab9535830a4/c.html) — state news agency, Chinese). In the "high-price buyback of old household items" livestream scam announced by Zhengzhou's anti-fraud center, a 75-year-old was cheated out of retirement savings by "your grain ration coupons (China's obsolete planned-economy ration tickets, now collector nostalgia items) are worth well over 100,000 yuan — just pay the 14,000-yuan (about US$1,900) handling fee first" ([Zhengzhou municipal government](https://www.zhengzhou.gov.cn/news1/10292256.jhtml) — official notice, Chinese). Different methods. Exactly the same chain.

## 2. The Six High-Frequency Scripts (Ranked by How Often They Come Knocking)

1. **Free eggs → supplement "treatment courses."** The #304 teardown calls this playbook "exponential fission": collect free eggs, join a WeChat group → shills posting "testimonials" in the group → time-limited flash sales of "expert formulas" in the livestream room. The endgame is getting the elder to stop taking their real medication.
2. **Collectibles / high-price buyback of old household items.** "This old family stamp is worth 500,000 yuan — just pay the auction service fee first." Any "windfall" that requires paying money before receiving money is a script.
3. **High-yield retirement projects.** Eldercare-bed leasebacks, "eco-estate" pre-IPO shares, high-interest deposits — all of these are illegal fundraising at bottom, and the principal hitting zero is only a matter of time.
4. **Impersonating a child or grandchild in trouble.** "Dad, I've been in a car crash, transfer the surgery fee now." In an era when even a single WeChat voice message may be AI-synthesized, a **family code word** is the only thing you can rely on.
5. **Fake police claiming you're "involved in a case."** Fake police ID + fake arrest warrant + "safe account." Elders hold the highest trust in uniforms — which is precisely this script's bullseye (for a similar case announced by People's Daily Online, see the [anti-fraud center case file](https://xj.people.com.cn/n2/2021/0907/c394722-34903500.html) — Chinese).
6. **Twilight romance / companionship pig-butchering.** Widowed elders are high-value pig-butchering targets: six months building affection, then one harvest and gone. The children are usually the last to know.

## 3. Defensive Moves: Five Things You Can Finish Saying at the Dinner Table

**Move 1: Install + authorize.**
Put the National Anti-Fraud Center app (China's official state anti-fraud app) on your parents' phones, switch on incoming-call alerts, and turn off "silence unknown callers" so the warnings can actually get through. This is system-level interception — more reliable than any amount of reminding.

**Move 2: Agree on a code word.**
Set a question-and-answer with the family that only insiders know ("What kind of tree is the one in the old family courtyard?"). For any "family member in trouble needs money" or "friend asking on a family member's behalf," check the code word first. One sentence dismantles script #4.

**Move 3: Set house rules — the three don't-touches.**
- "High yields" that come to you unsolicited: don't touch (retirement projects, pre-IPO shares);
- "Good deals" that require paying before receiving: don't touch (collectibles buybacks, refund and tax-return processing);
- Anything that comes with "don't tell the kids": tell the kids first (the "isolation" blade from the Five Blades essay, #330, is present in every elder-targeting script).

**Move 4: Control the entry points.**
- Have elders peel off shipping labels and destroy them before throwing out packages (personal data is the raw material of Link 1 — see #220);
- No scanning QR codes for free eggs, no filling out "free health check" registration forms, no buying anything from livestream "experts";
- A house rule for large transfers: **any amount above X yuan means one phone call to the kids first** — not for approval, but to add a "must pass the family" gate. The overwhelming majority of scams retreat right there.

**Move 5: Talk often — and not only about "don't get scammed."**
Scammers refresh their scripts weekly; your warnings get refreshed once a year. What works is talking about concrete cases: "they just arrested a gang selling supplements over livestream — the case involved ten million yuan." Stories stick where sermons slide off. The person an elder thinks to call first is the one who usually talks these things over with them.

## 4. If It Has Already Happened

- **Don't blame.** After being scammed, the two things elders fear most are losing face and disappointing their children; concealment is the single biggest source of losses. The first sentence should always be: "This is not your fault — the scammers are professionals."
- **The golden half-hour:** dial 110 or 96110 (110 is China's police emergency line; 96110 is the national anti-fraud hotline) for an emergency payment freeze, then go to the local police station to file a report, bringing every transfer receipt and chat log (#327 has the full procedure). Readers outside China: report fraud to your local police or national fraud-reporting service, e.g. IC3.gov in the US.
- **Preserve evidence:** keep the supplement products and their packaging, screen-record the livestream, keep every receipt — illegal fundraising and elder-fraud cases are usually organized operations, so the earlier the report, the faster the cases link together, and the higher the share of funds recovered.
- **Guard against the second harvest:** "pay us and we'll recover your losses," "we know insiders who can close the case" — all second-layer scams that specifically target families who have already filed reports.

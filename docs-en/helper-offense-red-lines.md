# Helper-Offense Red Lines: Which "Small Favor" Puts Criminal Liability on Your Back

> Long-form essay · Synthesized from 45 teardowns in the "money laundering · bank cards" category and 12 in the "job hunting · franchising" category. Written for everyone who has ever felt the pull of "easily make a few hundred yuan a day."

113 teardowns in this book carry the ⛓️ criminal-liability flag, and the largest group among them is not "how scammers deceive you" but **"how you became the scammer without noticing."** The helper offense (帮信罪) — formally, the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities, under Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II — is the charge most of them end in. This essay answers only three questions: where the red lines are, why people cross them, and what happens if you do.

## 1. Where the Red Lines Are: Seven "Small Favors"

Spread out every criminal-liability teardown on this site, and the ways an ordinary person can cross the line come down to these seven:

| # | The "small favor" | The charge you actually face | The standard pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Renting out or selling your bank cards or SIM cards | Helper offense, at minimum | "The card just sits there anyway — 300 yuan (about US$42) a day to rent it out" |
| 2 | Using your payment QR code or bank card to "receive money on someone's behalf" | Helper offense → concealment offense | "E-commerce order payments collected for you — you keep a 1% cut" |
| 3 | Part-time "money muling" (跑分), arbitraging transfer quota | Helper offense → concealment offense | "A few taps on your phone, commission paid daily" |
| 4 | "Lead generation" for scammers: building groups, posting ads, adding friends | Helper offense / fraud accomplice | "Likers wanted, street QR-code promotion, customer-service gigs" |
| 5 | Setting up GOIP rigs or SIM banks, or lending your phone for someone to "operate remotely" | Helper offense / fraud accomplice | "Host the equipment, collect rent lying down" |
| 6 | Selling personal data: shipping labels, résumés, homeowner lists | Infringement of citizens' personal information | "Buying data — 0.5 yuan (about US$0.07) a record" |
| 7 | Collecting or converting criminal proceeds: cash-for-USDT swaps, buying gift cards for redemption | Concealment offense | "Recharge for others, pocket the spread" |

Note item 4 — **lead generation is the link in this supply chain closest to ordinary people.** Among the 56 "part-time · order-brushing" teardowns in this book, a large share of "liker" and "reception customer-service" gigs are, in reality, lead-generation work for fraud gangs: you think you're doing an honest job, while the other end of your payslip is a telecom-fraud victim.

## 2. Why People Cross: Three Illusions

**Illusion one: "I only provided a card; I didn't cheat anyone out of money."**
In law this is called "providing payment-and-settlement assistance." You do not need to know where the money came from — as long as you "should have known" (the card was rented to a stranger, the commission is obviously abnormal, the other party communicates in code), the helper offense is established. In three helper-offense cases concluded by the Ma'anshan Intermediate Court, among 8 defendants the largest single money-muling flow reached 4.39 million yuan (about US$610,000), drawing a one-year sentence ([China Court Network](https://www.chinacourt.org/article/detail/2023/08/id/7492928.shtml) — official courts portal, Chinese). In a case from the Jieyang Intermediate Court, a person who lent out bank cards "for a little pocket money" was ultimately convicted and sentenced for concealment of criminal proceeds ([Legal Daily](https://www.legaldaily.com.cn/Lawyer/content/2024-09/20/content_9057983.html) — official legal newspaper, Chinese).

**Illusion two: "I'm just a student / stay-at-home mom / job seeker — I don't even have a credit record to ruin. What's to fear?"**
The helper offense is a **criminal record**, not an administrative penalty. It travels with you: civil-service exams, military enlistment, political vetting, and professional licensing in certain industries are all affected. And the defendant profile for this crime is strikingly concentrated — according to announcements from prosecutors' offices in multiple regions, **students and fresh-graduate job seekers are the hardest-hit group**, and the trigger is often nothing more than a "300 yuan a day, buying cards/codes" part-time ad.

**Illusion three: "There's no money in the card; if anything happens, they can't trace it to me."**
Every illicit yuan really does flow through your account. When police trace backward along the money chain, the first name they reach is the cardholder. And it doesn't stop at the criminal law — the companion sanctions of the "card cut-off" campaign (断卡, China's crackdown on fraud-linked bank cards and SIM cards) bar you from opening new cards for 5 years, suspend non-counter banking services, and restrict payment accounts ("card cut-off sanctions," see the glossary). A few hundred yuan of "rent" in exchange for five years of financial semi-paralysis: the math never works.

## 3. What Happens If You Do: The Ladder of Liability

Criminal liability is not a cliff. It's a slide. From lightest to heaviest:

1. **Administrative punishment**: minor circumstances, timely course correction, cooperation with the investigation — it may stop at detention or fines;
2. **The helper offense** (Criminal Law Art. 287-II): knowingly providing help while others use information networks to commit crimes — up to three years;
3. **The concealment offense** (Criminal Law Art. 312): transferring or hiding proceeds known to be from crime — up to seven years. Money muling with large flows, deep involvement, or fat cuts slides down to here;
4. **Fraud accomplice**: taking part in the scripts, the lead generation, or pulling people into the scam's core machinery — punished as the scammer's equal. In the pig-butchering case announced by the Supreme People's Procuratorate, five people across the whole chain, from "chat operators" to "desk managers," drew sentences of five-and-a-half to twelve years ([Supreme People's Procuratorate](https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dxwlzp2021/202105/t20210518_518545.shtml) — China's top prosecutorial body, Chinese).

The slide has a rule: **the more you know, the more you take, the longer you stay — the heavier the charge.** And nearly everyone who ever slid down started with a step too small to be worth mentioning.

## 4. Four Weld Points in the Defense Line

1. **Cards and QR codes never leave your hands** — no renting, no selling, no "just run a payment through it for a friend." Outside your spouse, parents, and children: refuse, across the board;
2. **"Collect payments for others / move money through your account / take a cut" = red-line phrases.** See any of the three in a part-time ad: swipe past it and report it;
3. **Your phone is never remotely operated by anyone** — "device hosting" and "earn while idle" are all dark-industry pitches;
4. **Self-audit regularly**: use "One-ID Lookup" (the official service listing every phone card and internet account registered under your ID) and "One-Click Card Check" (bank accounts under your name) (#328). Any card you didn't apply for: cancel it immediately — it may be committing crimes for someone else right now.

## 5. What to Do If You've Already Crossed

- Stop immediately: take back the cards/accounts and freeze the linked payment accounts;
- Go to the police proactively and explain the situation — voluntary surrender, returning proceeds, and assisting the case are statutory mitigating factors; the earlier, the better;
- Keep every chat log and transfer receipt — they are your proof that you "did not know";
- Never believe "pay to close the case" or "insiders who can pull strings" — that is the second-layer scam, aimed squarely at people whose case has already surfaced.

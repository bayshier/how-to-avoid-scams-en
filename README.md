## ⚠️ Read this first — the three rules

1. **Never click unfamiliar links; never share verification codes.** The one-time code is the last lock on your account. No legitimate support agent will ever ask for it, or ask you to install remote-control software.
2. **Any job that asks you to pay first is a scam.** Deposits, training fees, unfreezing fees — the more fee names they invent, the faster they run. Real employers don't charge their employees.
3. **Answer the anti-fraud call; call the police fast.** *(In China: answer 96110 — the national anti-fraud hotline calling YOU means you are being scammed right now; already transferred money? Call 110 immediately — freezing within 30 minutes gives the best chance of recovery. Readers outside China: report to your local police or national fraud-reporting service, e.g. [IC3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov) in the US.)*

## What this book is

Not a novel, not a textbook — a **field manual of how scams actually work**:

- **One scam = one story.** 405 teardowns in the original; each opens with one line on what the scam is after.
- **Every story follows the same skeleton.** The script in numbered steps (1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣), where the trap is (⚠️), how to defend (✅), which law applies (⚖️), and a one-line wake-up call (🛑).
- **Three tags per story.** ① Scam category (16 categories) ② What you lose (money? privacy? criminal liability?) ③ Who it targets (the elderly? students? merchants?).
- **Every story links to real cases.** The original carries 1,601 references to public notices from courts, procuratorates, police and state media — same scam type, real cases.

In other words: **you don't read it cover to cover. Something happens, you search the keyword.**

## The Scammer's Five Moves

If you read one thing, read [**five-moves.md**](docs-en/five-moves.md): after 335 teardowns, the pattern is that **scams change costumes but the psychological manipulation always uses the same five moves** — Fear, Authority, Greed, Urgency, and (the quietest) Isolation — new skin, same knife. Each move: what it is, what it looks like in real cases, and the exact step where you hang up.

## How this edition works

- China-specific concepts are glossed on first use (order-brushing scams, "helper offenses", pig-butchering, the 96110 hotline…). References to Chinese courts and state media keep their original links with short English notes.
- Story numbers (#NNN) match the Chinese original, so you can cross-reference the full text upstream.
- The upstream repo also ships a Chinese-only offline HTML reader, a self-check quiz, and a scam-detection game — all out of scope for this English edition; use the upstream repo for those.

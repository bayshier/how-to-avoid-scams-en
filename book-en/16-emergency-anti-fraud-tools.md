## #327 · If You Really Do Get Scammed: The Golden Half-Hour Self-Rescue Protocol — Don't Wait a Single Second

`Emergency & anti-fraud tools` `Anti-fraud toolbox` `💸 Money loss`

I hope you never need this piece. But read it to the end anyway. After a scam, every second, your money is moving at speed through the scammer's accounts — report one second faster, and the odds of recovery rise a notch.

- 1️⃣ **Call 110 (China's police emergency number) or 96110 (China's national anti-fraud hotline) immediately.**
Tell the operator plainly: "I've been defrauded by telecom/online fraud." Give three things: the amount lost, the scam type (fake customer service? order-brushing (fake e-commerce task/rebate scams)?), and the scammer's receiving account and the transfer time. Police will immediately trigger an emergency payment-stop and freeze the involved accounts — the golden window for payment stops is 48 hours; the earlier you call, the more money gets frozen.

- 2️⃣ **Call your bank at the same time.**
Dial your bank's customer service in parallel and request a temporary loss-report or payment stop on your own bank cards. If a password leaked, change it immediately. If you were talked into taking online loans, contact each platform's customer service at once, explain the situation, and keep records.

- 3️⃣ **Preserve every piece of evidence.**
Transfer records (bank, WeChat, Alipay screenshots), chat logs, call logs, the other party's accounts, the fraud app and URLs — screenshot and save all of it; delete nothing! Many people quietly delete chat records out of embarrassment, which makes the later investigation many times harder.

- 4️⃣ **Give a statement at the nearest police station.**
Bring the evidence to the nearest station (scammed while traveling? Filing locally works too — China's anti-fraud system operates across jurisdictions). Cooperate with the interview record. After police secure the electronic evidence, follow their instructions for handling the fraud app and links on your phone.

> ❌ The biggest second trap: "cyber-cops," "hackers," and "recovery teams" that pop up online claiming that, for a fee, they can get your money back — the desperation to claw it back is exactly the prey the next wave of scammers is hunting. Anyone charging money to "recover your losses" is, one hundred percent, running a second scam.

> ⚠️ Never skip reporting because "the amount is small" or "it's embarrassing": every report is a lead police use to link cases and strike the entire chain.

> 🛡️ Stop sending the other party a single further yuan, right now. The moment something feels wrong, cutting losses matters ten thousand times more than winning it back.

> 🛡️ Rescue your emotions too: being scammed is not your fault; scammers are professional criminal organizations. Talk to your family, and if needed call a psychological-support hotline. Never carry it alone or do anything extreme.

> ⚖️ Article 64 of China's Criminal Law: all property illegally obtained by criminals shall be recovered, and a victim's lawful property shall be returned promptly. Reporting is nothing to be ashamed of — it is the only legitimate route to getting your money back.

> 🛑 Memorize the three "right aways": call 110 right away; get your bank to stop payments right away; save the evidence right away.

## #328 · Seven Anti-Fraud Tools the State Issued You — and Most People Have Used None

`Emergency & anti-fraud tools` `Anti-fraud toolbox` `🔓 Privacy breach`

Anti-fraud is not just "be more careful." The state has actually issued equipment to everyone. All seven tools below are free; use three of them, and roughly eight in ten scams will break off before they ever reach you.

- 1️⃣ **The National Anti-Fraud Center app (China's official anti-scam app).**
Not a publicity app — a fraud firewall inside your phone. Turn on "call warnings" and suspected scam calls trigger an automatic alert; "app self-check" scans your phone for hidden trojans; you can also look up suspicious accounts, URLs, and payment accounts, and report fraud leads with one tap. Download it only from the official app store, and enable the warnings under your real name.

- 2️⃣ **The 96110 warning-and-intervention hotline (China's national anti-fraud hotline).**
This is the police calling you. When 96110 rings, you or a family member is being scammed — answer, and hear it through; the call is there to save your money. You can also dial 96110 yourself whenever you're unsure.

- 3️⃣ **12381 fraud-warning SMS.**
A warning channel pushed by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. If a 12381 text arrives, you may be in a scammer's crosshairs — stop every "transfer operation" in progress immediately.

- 4️⃣ **"One-ID lookup" for SIM cards.**
Using your own national ID, you can check how many phone SIM cards are registered in your name nationwide. Cards you don't know about? Someone may have impersonated you to open them — deal with it now, before the police come looking.

- 5️⃣ **"One-ID lookup 2.0" for internet accounts.**
With your phone number and the last six digits of your ID, check how many internet accounts are registered to that number — and catch accounts fraudulently opened in your name before they're used.

- 6️⃣ **UnionPay's "one-tap card check" (via the UnionPay app, Yunshanfu).**
Check with one tap how many bank cards you hold across every bank. Extra cards you never opened may have been rented out by "card farmers" (people who sell or rent bank cards to crime rings) to run fraud money — if a card is implicated, yours is the first account frozen.

- 7️⃣ **The anti-fraud caller badge.**
The Ministry of Public Security's intervention calls carry an official verified badge — the screen shows "National Anti-Fraud Center · Police Officer." See the badge, and answer without worry.

> ❌ Some think "installing an anti-fraud app means my privacy is being monitored" — quite the opposite: it doesn't read your chats; it only screens for fraud patterns. Not installing it is leaving the front door wide open.

> ⚠️ No anti-fraud app can stop you from willingly transferring money to your "online romance partner." The tools are a shield; don't walk into the spear yourself.

> 🛡️ Install and configure everything on your parents' phones, then teach them to recognize 96110. That set of actions is worth a Lunar New Year red envelope.

> ⚖️ Renting out, selling, or lending three or more SIM cards or bank cards brings official disciplinary measures and possible liability for the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities (Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II — selling or renting your bank card or SIM card can make YOU criminally liable). Find extra cards under your name? Cancel them immediately and report to the police.

> 🛑 The seven tools in one line: install the app, answer 96110, recognize 12381, check your SIM cards, check your internet accounts, check your bank cards, trust the anti-fraud caller badge.

## #27 · Just been scammed? Seize the "golden 30 minutes" to maximize your recovery

`Money Mules & Bank Cards` `⛓️ Criminal-Liability Risk` `💸 Money Lost`

Whether it's ten thousand, fifty thousand, or hundreds of thousands — even tens of millions — most people's first reaction after being scammed online is despair. Since the vast majority of scammers sit overseas and move money through layers of accounts, recovery is genuinely difficult. It is not hopeless!

If you've been scammed, force yourself calm right now and execute the steps below to seize the high ground for getting your money back.

**⏱️ Seize the "golden 30 minutes": the core of damage control**

Your single best window to recover funds is within 30 minutes of the transfer. Time is the safety of your money — fight for every second:

- Stop all transfers immediately 🛑: cut every channel of contact with the scammer. Never believe talk of an "unfreeze fee," a "margin deposit," or a "second unfreeze"!
- Preserve all evidence 📸: screenshot and keep transfer records, chat logs, links, and the scammer's receiving account numbers and account names.
- Call 110 (China's police emergency number) and request an "emergency payment stop" 📞: explicitly ask the police for an emergency stop-payment (a temporary freeze) and clearly provide the scammer's receiving account details! (Readers outside China: report fraud to your local police or national fraud-reporting service, e.g. IC3.gov in the US.)
- Go to the police and file a report 🚔: give your statement, and make sure you walk out with the case-acceptance receipt.

> 💡 Why an "emergency payment stop"?
> As long as the money is still sitting in the receiving account, an emergency stop-payment instantly "locks" that account (deposits allowed, withdrawals blocked), so the proceeds cannot be swept away. Even if the funds have already moved on to second- or third-tier accounts, police can follow the chain and intercept layer by layer.

**⚖️ Hold the black-industry "tool people" liable: card owners must pay compensation**

The scammers move money so smoothly only because of a domestic corps of "two-card people" — black-industry tool people who sell or rent out their own bank cards, WeChat Pay or Alipay accounts.

- They are not innocent ⚠️: for commissions of a few hundred yuan, they rent and sell accounts they know full well will be used for criminal gray industries.
- The legal consequences are severe 🔨: the conduct constitutes suspected "bangxin zui" — the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities (Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II).
- The breakthrough for recovering your loss 💰: your money went straight into these card owners' accounts, and they are seriously at fault. Facing them is not just an order to return proceeds and pay compensation, with heavy fines on top, but prison. Pursuing the card owners for civil compensation alongside their criminal liability is a major route to getting your money back.

> 🛡️ Summary: the defense-and-response rules
> Before the fact: never trust online order-brushing ("order-brushing" = fake e-commerce task/rebate scams), high-yield investing, fake mentors, or unfreeze tricks.
> After the fact: act decisively — report to police at once + emergency payment stop + preserve evidence, and use the law to fight to the end.

## #141 · Owners of tobacco-and-liquor shops and jewelry stores, take note: beware the "big order" money-laundering trap

`Money Mules & Bank Cards` `💸 Money Lost` `🎯 Shop Owners`

👉 A deep teardown of the "big purchase + third-party payment / direct transfer + goods-to-cash" laundering chain: recognize the fake big customer and the real money laundering, and protect your account from freezing and your payment from confiscation.

If you run a tobacco-and-liquor shop, a gold or jewelry store, a consumer-electronics store, or an e-bike dealership, raise your guard!

Black-industry crews have recently been targeting brick-and-mortar shops with high single-ticket values and easily resold goods, turning merchants into "money-laundering relay stations" for telecom-fraud proceeds! 💸

Today we expose the real mechanics of "big-order laundering" and the anti-trap guide.

**🕵️‍♂️ The 2 core plays of "big-order laundering"**

- 1️⃣ Third-party QR-code payment 📈
Black-industry operators walk in and buy large quantities of liquor, cigarettes, or gold, then claim their phone balance is insufficient or their payment limit is capped, asking a "friend" or "company finance" to pay remotely. In reality, the person actually paying is a telecom-fraud victim! The moment that victim reports to the police, your receiving account and the funds are lawfully frozen by public security.
- 2️⃣ Large direct bank transfer 🪤
The crew poses as a big customer, orders tens or even hundreds of thousands of yuan in high-value goods at once, and asks for your bank account number for a direct transfer. The merchant, thrilled at the big order, ships in a hurry — but after the victim reports, the police trace the tainted funds straight to your door. The "payment" you received is then confiscated as criminal proceeds, and your bank card — and every yuan in it — can be frozen for months or even years!

> 🛡️ Merchant anti-trap guide & safety line
> 🔒 Real name, same person: strictly follow "whoever buys the goods pays, real names matched" — refuse any third-party payment whatsoever!
> 💵 Verify large transfers: when an unknown big customer wants to pay by direct bank transfer, verify their true identity and the source of funds, and keep every transaction record.
> ⚠️ High-risk, easy-to-resell sectors: any trade with single transactions above ¥50,000 in easily resold goods (gold, liquor and cigarettes, electronics) is a high-frequency money-laundering zone!
> 🛑 Rather lose the sale than get played: faced with a suspicious deal — "no bargaining, ship urgently, third-party payment" — walking away beats becoming the launderer's tool!

Running a physical shop means hard-earned money. Don't chase a big order, lose your principal, and gamble away your account security too.
Forward this to friends who run shops or are starting a business — warn them in time! 👇

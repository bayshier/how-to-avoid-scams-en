## #331 · iPhone users, take note: one FaceTime video call, and an "officer" puts on a fake police uniform

`Impersonation · Police & Courts` `⛓️Criminal-liability risk` `Impersonated calls`

Apple users should pay attention to one feature right now: FaceTime. Scammers discovered it lets them freely change the displayed caller name — and turned it into a new fraud channel. Set the name to "XX City Public Security Bureau," swap in a police-badge avatar, place a video call, and there on your screen sits an "officer in uniform."

1️⃣ The display name is editable, so any "official" can be faked
FaceTime lets users set their own display name. Changing it to "XX Police" or "XX Anti-Fraud Center" takes zero effort — even the Hangzhou police have been impersonated this way.

2️⃣ A "police uniform" and an "arrest warrant" appear on video
The caller wears a "uniform," gives a name and badge number, and shows a "Criminal Arrest Warrant" bearing your own ID number. They claim you are implicated in money laundering and demand you cooperate with an "online investigation" and download a designated app — screen sharing becomes their "bodycam."

3️⃣ "Clearing your name" requires a transfer
You are told to provide your bank cards for a "fund audit" and move your money into a "safe account" to prove your innocence, with repeated reminders that "the case is confidential — you may tell no one." One victim discovered over ¥70,000 gone from her card three hours later.

4️⃣ Same channel, new costume
Over the same channel they also run "WeChat customer service here to cancel your 'Million Guarantee' charge" (a free payment-protection feature scammers falsely claim is about to start billing) and "bank customer service closing your loan account." The channel changes; the script never does.

> ❌ Police, prosecutors, and courts do not handle cases by phone, video, or "online." There is no "safe account," and they will never ask you to transfer money to "prove your innocence."
> ⚠️ How to tell them apart: a normal call shows the caller's region at the top and a phone icon to answer; a FaceTime call shows "FaceTime Video" at the top, a live front-camera image as the background, and a camera icon to answer.
> 🛡️ Hang up on any FaceTime call from a stranger. If you never use the feature, open iPhone Settings → FaceTime → turn it off.
> 🛡️ For any call claiming to be from police or platform customer service, call back through official channels to verify. Never trust caller ID.
> ⚖️ Impersonating a state-functionary to deceive is the crime of imposture, punished more severely; extracting money this way additionally constitutes fraud.
> 🛑 Help the older people in your family turn off FaceTime. The move takes ten seconds — someone in their neighborhood has already fallen for it.

## #332 · "Turn on screen sharing and I'll walk you through it" — what you're sharing isn't your screen, it's your bank card

`Impersonation · Police & Courts` `Impersonated calls`

"No worries if you're not good with this — I'll assist you remotely. Download this meeting app, turn on screen sharing, and I'll show you step by step."
Hear that sentence? Hang up immediately. From the moment you tap "Share Screen," everything on your phone is being broadcast live to the scammer.

1️⃣ How they get clairvoyance
Once screen sharing is on, every frame of your screen streams to them in real time: the bank password you type, every SMS verification code you receive, your balance, your credit line — all visible.

2️⃣ Why verification codes fall anyway
You think a code is safe as long as you never "tell" them? Wrong. With screen sharing on, the SMS pop-up displays right on your screen — the scammer reads it perfectly well without you ever saying it aloud.

3️⃣ The script never varies
Fake customer service "processing your refund" asks to share; fake bank staff "closing your account" asks to share; fake police "auditing your assets" asks to share; order-brushing "mentors guiding your tasks" ask to share. Anyone who asks you to enable screen sharing is a scammer — no exceptions.

4️⃣ Exiting properly matters
When something feels wrong, returning to the home screen or switching apps is NOT enough — the share may keep running in the background. The correct move is to tap "Stop Sharing" inside the meeting app; if the phone is already under remote control, force a shutdown or pull the SIM card to cut losses.

> ❌ "Screen sharing won't affect your funds" and "this is just a normal video-meeting feature" — every such line exists to lower your guard.
> ⚠️ The generic version disguises itself as a "collaboration assistant," "remote guidance," or "after-sales support," and the apps really are downloadable from app stores — highly convincing.
> 🛡️ Never enable screen sharing with a stranger, under any circumstances. Real customer service, police, and banks will never ask for it.
> 🛡️ If the caller steers you toward "operating your phone while we talk," end the call at once and verify through official customer service or 96110.
> ⚖️ Using screen sharing to steal verification codes and money constitutes theft or fraud; developing or distributing remote-control tools for fraud constitutes the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities (Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II).
> 🛑 Remember one sentence: the moment your screen is shared, your passwords and codes are running naked. The two words "Stop Sharing" can save your savings at the critical moment.

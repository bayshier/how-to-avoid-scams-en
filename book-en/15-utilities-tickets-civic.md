## #320 · The "Flight Cancelled" Call That Knows Your Seat Number: Two Cards Emptied in Ten Minutes

`Utilities, tickets & civic services` `Daily-life scam` `💸 Money loss` `🔓 Privacy breach`

The day before your flight, an international call starting with "00" (China's international dialing prefix) comes in. The caller claims to be "airline customer service" and recites your name, ID number, flight number, and departure time without a single error — then, full of apology: the flight has been cancelled due to a mechanical fault, the company will rebook you proactively, and you'll be paid ¥300 in delay insurance.

Every detail correct, every tone professional and considerate. Would you believe it? Countless travelers nationwide already have — and within ten to twenty minutes their cards are emptied, including online-loan credit lines they never knew they had, drawn down and swept away.

- 1️⃣ **Perfect data up front; impersonating official customer service.**
The scammer first buys your booking data on the black market (court verdicts already exist against both airline insiders and ticketing-company leaks). On the call they recite your name, flight number, and seat without a flaw — and your guard collapses on the spot.

- 2️⃣ **Compensation offered first: a taste of sweetness.**
"Cancellation due to mechanical failure" or "severe weather," with rebooking plus payout offered proactively. Note this detail: the scammer may even have you run one refund attempt on the legitimate platform first, stage a "refund failed" screen, and let you conclude that you truly need "manual assistance."

- 3️⃣ **Getting you to install software and switch on screen sharing.**
"The claim procedure is complicated; let me assist you remotely" — a link arrives, pushing you to download a video-conference app like "Collaboration Assistant," open a page impersonating the civil-aviation authority, and enable screen sharing. From that moment, every character you type and every verification code that pops up is visible to the scammer in real time.

- 4️⃣ **The verification-code chain: savings first, then loans.**
Under the pretext of "confirming identity" and "verifying the payout channel," they make you read out SMS verification codes one by one. Once your cards are empty, they use "the claim channel must verify your repayment capacity" to talk you into borrowing on every online-loan platform you can reach — until every credit line is squeezed dry.

> ❌ "If you don't claim the compensation it will affect your credit record" / "The claim channel closes today" — manufactured panic and urgency are standard issue on every rebooking scam.

> ⚠️ The most dangerous "customer service" calls come one or two days before departure — precisely because you're anxious to travel and afraid of missing the flight.

> 🛡️ One iron rule: a real agent will never have you install software, switch on screen sharing, read out verification codes, or reveal your card password. Refunds only return the way the money came in — "refund where you bought it."

> 🛡️ Received a "flight cancelled" or "delay compensation" message? Never call the number back. Verify through the airline's official hotline, official app, or booking platform; if in doubt, call 96110 (China's national anti-fraud hotline) for advice.

> ⚖️ Impersonating airline customer service to steal money through fake refund-and-rescheduling claims constitutes fraud under Article 266 of China's Criminal Law; ¥3,000 already meets the "relatively large amount" prosecution threshold. Airline and ticketing "insiders" who resell passenger data face separate, more heavily punished charges of infringing citizens' personal information.

> 🛑 If screen sharing is already on and your phone is out of your control, force a shutdown or pull the SIM card immediately to stop the bleeding. The moment you realize you've been scammed, call 110 (China's police emergency number) at once — police can place an emergency freeze on the involved accounts. One traveler who lost ¥430,000 recovered every yuan because they reported fast.

## #321 · "Your Package Was Lost — Triple Compensation"? The Payout Never Comes; Your Card Is Emptied First

`Utilities, tickets & civic services` `Daily-life scam` `💸 Money loss` `🔓 Privacy breach`

Your package hasn't shown a tracking update in three days. Just as you're getting anxious, the phone rings: "Hello, I'm a claims specialist with XX Express. Your package was lost in transit; per company policy we'll compensate you three times its value."

They recite your tracking number, your name, your delivery address — all correct. Since the package really hasn't arrived, you follow along — and then the money in your card is gone.

- 1️⃣ **Hunting people whose logistics are already failing.**
Scammers single out users with delayed or stranded packages and time the call to when the item is urgent. The data is precise, and disbelief becomes hard.

- 2️⃣ **Proactive compensation; then "add me on WeChat."**
"It's hard to explain on the phone — add me on WeChat and I'll walk you through it step by step." Once you've added them, they send a QR code or a link: "the claims channel."

- 3️⃣ **A fake official site; you fill in everything.**
The code opens a "courier company website" nearly identical to the real one. The form asks for: name, ID number, bank card number, card password, the balance in the card — and, last, the SMS verification code.

- 4️⃣ **"Code entered incorrectly" — charged again and again.**
The first time you submit the code, the page says "incorrect, please re-enter." You didn't mistype anything. The error message is the scammer's device to make you enter codes repeatedly so multiple charges can go through. By the time the deduction texts arrive one after another, they have blocked you and disappeared.

> ❌ "Unclaimed compensation expires automatically" / "Past the deadline it counts as forfeited" — every claim that rushes you is trying to stop you from thinking.

> ⚠️ One variant of the same script: you receive a "delivery failed, parcel returned" text and call back. This time the parcel is supposedly a "Million Guarantee" insurance policy, and if you don't cancel today, ¥20,000 a month will be deducted. ("Million Guarantee" is in fact a free, built-in WeChat Pay protection feature — there is no policy and no fee.)

> 🛡️ To check whether a package is truly lost, use the shopping platform's or the courier's official app and official customer-service number. Never click links from strange texts or from any "claims specialist."

> 🛡️ The verification code is the last lock on the door. Under no circumstances type it into someone else's webpage or read it out to any "customer service."

> ⚖️ Counterfeiting a courier website to steal bank-card data and drain accounts constitutes theft or fraud; those who build or maintain the phishing site are separately criminally liable for the crime of assisting information-network criminal activities (Chinese Criminal Law Art. 287-II — renting out or selling your bank card or SIM card to criminals can also make YOU criminally liable).

> 🛑 Memorize this sentence word for word: when a courier company genuinely pays compensation, the money returns directly, by the original payment route, to your account. No password and no verification code — ever.

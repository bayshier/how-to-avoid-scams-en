## #139 — Exposed: The "Pig-Butchering" Scam — the Dark Industry Chain and the Trap of Human Nature Behind It

`Investment · Crypto` `💸Financial loss` `🚫Account bans` `🎯Targets women`

👉 A deep teardown of the pig-butchering (romance-investment scam — the standard term in English media) assembly line: "build a persona → target precisely → pay out small → harvest big." Recognize the fake feelings behind the real harvesting, before the scam empties your life savings.

In the network's dark corners and underground trading circles, pig-butchering is packaged as a "low-risk, high-return" money-spinner. The operating logic isn't complicated, but its destructive earning power is astonishing: case amounts run from hundreds of thousands of yuan (¥) into the tens of millions. Today the whole dark playbook — and the defense guide — gets fully exposed. 💸

**🕵️‍♂️ The pig-butchering black industry: 4 assembly-line moves**

- 1️⃣ Building the perfect persona (identity, carefully packaged) 📈
The scammer invents identities — "tall, rich and handsome," "elite career woman," "successful investor" — stocked with private-jet, mansion, and luxury-goods photos as social-media material, packaging themselves as the perfect romantic or investment partner.
- 2️⃣ Precision-targeted seduction (hitting human weakness exactly) 🪤
They open with whatever the target lacks: those craving affection get meticulous, around-the-clock care; those craving wealth get a so-called "insider loophole / inside channel." A strong trust bond takes shape.
- 3️⃣ Small rebates to set the hook (a long line for a big fish) 💰
Early on, victims are guided to invest a few thousand yuan — and quickly receive real, withdrawable small returns, dissolving every last shred of caution.
- 4️⃣ The snowball harvest (squeeze out every asset) 🚨
Once trust is at maximum, victims are pushed to keep adding six- and seven-figure sums — even pressured into taking out loans or mortgaging assets to "recharge" — until the account is frozen, they're blocked, and the harvest is complete.

> ⚠️ The nature of the trap
🚨 Enormous damage: whether the target is a sharp entrepreneur, an influencer with millions of followers, or an ordinary office worker, once you're in, individual losses typically start in the hundreds of thousands of yuan — with no ceiling.

> 🛑 The takeaway — the iron rule of defense: anyone you meet online who warmly, attentively "helps you make money" and steers you into depositing, trading stocks, or trading crypto on an illegal platform is running a pig-butchering scam, 100% of the time. 🔒 No perfect romance or can't-lose investment falls out of the sky — never treat phantom affection as a life raft. See pig-butchering for what it is, protect your hard-earned money, and share this with the single friends and side-gig hunters around you before they become the next pig. 👇

## #172 — Beware USDT QR-Code Theft: Inside the Wallet "Malicious Approval" Trap

`Investment · Crypto` `💸Financial loss`

👉 A deep teardown of crypto "approval phishing": don't be blinded by bait like "above-market currency exchange" or "free airdrops," and don't end up with a wiped wallet and assets swept away in seconds.

"I only scanned one code to make one transfer — how did every USDT in my wallet vanish instantly?!" In crypto circles, many users have suffered this sudden emptying of assets. The technique behind it exploits no flaw in blockchain itself — it weaponizes the perfectly legitimate "smart-contract approval" feature of decentralized wallets to launch an extremely stealthy approval-phishing scam. Today the billing logic behind "QR-code coin theft" — and the defense guide — explained in one pass. 💸

**🕵️‍♂️ Deep teardown: the 4 core moves of "QR-code coin theft"**

- 1️⃣ Bait in disguise, luring you to scan or click 📈
Scammers dangle "above-market private currency exchange, over-the-counter trade discounts, free trending airdrops/perks," then send the victim a malicious QR code or phishing link.
- 2️⃣ The bait-and-switch: dressed up as an ordinary transfer 🪤
When the victim scans the code with a decentralized wallet (TokenPocket, imToken, MetaMask, etc.) to pay, the confirmation screen is disguised as an ordinary small transfer — highly deceptive.
- 3️⃣ Confirmation trap: the "malicious approval" is completed 🔑
The victim believes they're paying a few dollars in fees or a small invoice — but entering the password to confirm actually executes an instruction opening spending rights on their wallet for the scammer's contract address (Approve / authorization)!
- 4️⃣ Remote drain: assets swept away without you feeling a thing 💰
Once the approval succeeds, the scammer holds standing permission to call specific tokens in your wallet at any time. A background script then initiates the remote pull and moves every USDT out instantly — no second password, no confirmation from you.

> ⚠️ The brutal truth behind "approval theft"
> ❌ Not a blockchain bug — you handed over the keys yourself: scammers abuse the normal "approval mechanism" in token standards such as ERC-20 / TRC-20. Blockchain transactions are public, transparent, and irreversible; once you sign the approval, neither law enforcement nor the wallet provider can force a reversal.
💳 As long as the approval stands, you can be robbed again anytime: while the malicious approval remains live, any USDT you later deposit into that wallet is instantly detected and auto-swept by the black-industry script!

> 🛡️ Defense guide and security line
- 1️⃣ Never scan QR codes of unknown origin: refuse every private-exchange or scan-for-airdrop temptation; never scan a stranger's so-called "payment code / registration code."
- 2️⃣ Check what the wallet popup actually is: before confirming a transaction, look hard — is it "Transfer," or "Approve / Unlimited allowance"? Any approval request aimed at an unfamiliar address: refuse, no exceptions.
- 3️⃣ Audit and revoke approvals regularly: use a trusted block explorer or tool (Revoke.cash, or the approval managers built into TP/imToken wallets) to review your wallet's approval records, and promptly cancel unnecessary or suspicious contract permissions. 💡

🛑 The takeaway: scams blanket the internet and scammers never run out — stay alert and hold the line. Never scan unknown codes, never approve unknown contracts; check every popup for "Transfer" vs. "Approve." Share this with the friends who live in decentralized wallets and play in Web3 — wake them up before it's their wallet on the drain list. 👇

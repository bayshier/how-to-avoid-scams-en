## #246 · The Shared Power Bank Trap: "Deposit-Free," "First 5 Minutes Free" — It's a Bloodsucking ATM

`Everyday-life traps` `🎯 Shop owners`

"Credit-based deposit waiver. Free to borrow. First 5 minutes free." — At certain shady shared-power-bank operators, these are not thoughtful services. They are bait, lowering you into the trap one step at a time! 💸

You think one hour of borrowing costs a few yuan? The final deduction is tens of yuan — sometimes ¥99.

A scheme built on artificially created return obstacles and word games to squeeze out deposits and overage fees is designed, from beginning to end, to make you pay more.

**🪤 Step One: Easy to Borrow, Hard to Return — The Obstacles Are Manufactured**

Legitimate rental services live and die by easy borrowing and returning. Certain shady operators do exactly the opposite: all they think about is how to keep you from returning the unit, run up the clock, and bill you more:

🏢 **Cabinets only where you borrow:** You can grab one at the mall entrance. When you come back to return it, you hunt for half an hour and find every nearby machine is full or powered off.

> 🛑 **Merchants maliciously "clog the slots":** Some shop staff plug every return slot full with their own power banks — borrowing only, never returning — leaving you nowhere to dock yours.

🤖 **Customer service, nearly unreachable:** You sprint around with a dying phone, sweat-soaked, while the meter jumps from ¥3 to ¥10 to ¥30. Want support? It's all chatbots. Want to appeal? The entry point is hidden better than an escape-room exit!

> 💰 **Step Two: Piling It On — From Overtime Fees to a High-Price Forced "Buyout"**

Overtime fees are small money. The forced buyout fee is where shady operators make their real profit!

🔋 **Paying a premium for e-waste:** When you can't return the unit, the system flags "auto-buyout on timeout" and deducts ¥99 on the spot. Do you have a no-name power bank at home that charges slowly, has a loose port, and is scratched all over? That's the "e-waste costing under ¥10" you were forced to buy last time.

☑️ **Pre-checked boxes skimming membership fees:** When you scan the code there's a tiny pre-checked option at the bottom of the page. Miss it, and ¥20 a month in "membership fees" auto-deducts!

> 🛡️ **Invisible charges stacked on top:** Every order quietly deducts ¥1–2 of "insurance." Unless you audit your statements line by line, you'll never notice. Skim millions of people a day, and that's over ¥10 million a month in pure profit!

📈 **Rigging the "capped billing" rule:** The "cap" is not the 24-hour cap you imagine — it's the operator's own high daily rate. Fail to return today, get charged once. Fail again tomorrow, get charged again!

> ⚖️ **Legal red lines, and how to protect yourself**

Forcing consumers who cannot return a device into a high-priced buyout amounts to coercive trading; deducting fees through pre-checked boxes amounts to consumer fraud. Under China's Consumer Rights Protection Law, consumers have the right to know the true nature of the service they're buying. Business models that hide key information and manufacture artificial obstacles for profit will eventually answer to the law.

> 💡 **The final wake-up**

What shady operators are really targeting is not the 20% "lifesaving charge" in your phone — it is your anxiety the moment the battery dies.

📱 **When you're out:** Carry your own light power bank, or charge for a while at a legitimate brick-and-mortar shop.

🙅‍♂️ **Be careful what you scan:** Don't scan codes just to save trouble. You think you're switching on a battery savior; you're actually switching on a silent, bloodsucking ATM!

Forward this to friends who regularly use shared power banks — stop donating money for free! ✨

## #267 · "Free" Is the Most Expensive: The ¥10,000 Installment-Loan Scheme Behind "Free Pet Adoption"

`Everyday-life traps` `💸 Money loss` `🎯 Students` `🎯 Shop owners`

"Short of money for a house or a car? Grab a batch of strays, hang up a 'free adoption' sign, and you can rake it in?"

Cat ownership keeps growing, and platforms like Xiaohongshu (a Chinese lifestyle social platform) and Xianyu (Alibaba's giant second-hand marketplace) are flooded with "free cat" and "free adoption" posts. Many green young people and college students think they've scored a free pedigree cat — and end up, without realizing it, carrying a "cat installment loan" of nearly ¥10,000. Then the cat dies of illness, and the monthly deductions continue without fail.

Today we strip the paint off this "scripted adoption" black-industry chain — dressed up as charity, built to harvest young people's goodwill. The logic is utterly shameless. Read this, and never step in it again.

**🎪 Act One: Building the Stage — a Pop-Up "Experience Hall" Dressed as Charity Adoption**

To harvest fast and be ready to bolt at any moment, scammers use an extremely asset-light model:

📍 **Short leases in prime shopping districts:** They rent a bare storefront in an area thick with young people — no renovation, short-term only. A few rows of cat cages, a big "FREE ADOPTION" banner.

🐱 **A wall of breeds, plus "requirements":** Ragdolls, golden and silver shaded British Shorthairs — every trendy pedigree cat fills the store. To look legitimate, they solemnly post "adoption requirements": you must be loving, have your family's consent, be an adult with stable income, and accept unscheduled follow-up visits.

🎯 **Precision targeting:** This deceptive "charity" packaging exists to trap young people who are short on money but desperate to own a pedigree cat.

**✍️ Act Two: The Chain of Traps — the Hidden Installment Loan Behind "We're Afraid You'll Abandon It"**

The moment you're holding a cat and can't let go, the clerk hands over the pre-written "adoption agreement" and starts tightening the screws:

> 📜 **The bait-and-switch "adoption agreement":** Invoking the need to "prevent abuse, abandonment, or resale of the cat," the clerk has you sign an agreement whose real core has nothing to do with abandonment. It obliges you to top up a fixed monthly "cat living fee" (¥200–300 at the low end, up to ¥1,000+) into the merchant's mini-program (an app that runs inside WeChat or Alipay) — locked in for one to two years!

**🌾 Overpriced, garbage "exclusive cat food":**

They condition you: "You'd be buying cat food every month anyway — buying it here is the same." In reality, what the merchant's mini-program sells is not a legitimate brand, and it costs more than double the mainstream big names. The food is mostly grain with virtually no meat, made palatable only by strong attractants — long-term feeding causes massive, irreversible damage to the cat.

**💳 Hidden installment loans like "Zhima Xianxiang" (Sesame Pay-Later, an Ant Group consumer-credit product):**

The most vicious part: the monthly "living fee" runs through hidden installment platforms like Zhima Xianxiang. This is, in substance, a disguised consumer loan! Once you sign the authorization, unless the merchant switches it off in their back office, the deduction fires every single month no matter what happens — and it is tied directly to your personal credit record (China's credit-scoring system).

**💀 Act Three: The Final Harvest — No Way Out, and the Despair Loop of the "Week Cat"**

You thought the monthly food payments were the end of it? Something even more vile comes next:

**💔 The "week cat" that dies days after you bring it home:**

These cats are mostly bought in bulk through illegal channels from other regions at rock-bottom prices, with no legitimate quarantine certificates at all; many are already dying en route. Within days of arriving home, they break out with feline panleukopenia ("cat plague") and other fast, deadly infectious diseases.

> 💸 **The cat dies; the loan goes on:** The agreement is stuffed with the adopter's liabilities and says not one word about the merchant's after-sale obligations. If the cat falls ill, you pay the heavy treatment costs. Even if the cat dies, the monthly "living fee" loan must still be repaid on schedule!

**☠️ Take the money and vanish:**

If you try to terminate early and settle in one payment, the merchant brazenly demands a ¥5,000–8,000 "buyout fee." And because they only rented the space short-term, by the time you react, the storefront is already empty. The cat is dead, the food stops shipping — but the automatic deductions continue in the background, and the merchant has blocked you on WeChat and by phone.

> ⚖️ **Final warning: "Free" is the most expensive!**

Run the numbers: a pedigree cat of ordinary quality from a legitimate channel costs about ¥1,000. In these script shops you'll sink ¥10,000 or more, all told.

In this black-industry model: cat cost (<5%) + no-name cat-food cost (30%) = merchant net profit (as high as 50–60%!)

What they exploit is precisely your itch for a bargain — and your goodwill and love for small animals.

> 🛑 **Anti-scam iron rules:**

Refuse any adoption that comes with tied spending or forced monthly deductions!

Before signing anything, read carefully for hidden clauses — installment plans, credit authorizations.

If you genuinely want to adopt, go through a local legitimate stray-animal shelter or nonprofit adoption platform.

Don't let your kindness become a cash pump for a scam ring!

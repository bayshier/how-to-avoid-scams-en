## #336 · The 2026 Scam Landscape Has Changed: 10 High-Frequency Types and 4 New Methods, Per the Ministry of Public Security — Check Which Ones Have Already Caught You

`Black & gray industry` `Shifts in the big board` `💸 Money loss`

At its anti-fraud press conference, the Ministry of Public Security (China's national police) reported that the ten most common telecom-fraud types today are: order-brushing rebate scams (fake e-commerce task/rebate scams), fake shopping-and-services scams, fake online investment schemes, impersonation of e-commerce and logistics customer service, loan and credit-record scams, fake in-game trading, online romance scams, impersonation of police/prosecutors/courts, impersonation of bosses or acquaintances, and flight-cancellation/rescheduling scams. Together they account for 85% of all telecom-fraud cases. Order-brushing rebates are the most frequent (25% of cases); fake investments cause the largest losses (40% of all money lost — nearly every case in the millions of yuan falls here). But more important than the list: the scammers have changed their equipment.

- 1️⃣ **The recruitment ground has moved: 66% of scams now start on short-video and social platforms.**
Scams used to begin with spam texts and nuisance calls. Now they "meet you naturally" inside messaging apps, short-video comment sections, and livestream DMs. A stranger striking up conversation, pulling you into a group, sending you a QR code — that is the new "unknown caller."

- 2️⃣ **Luring you onto niche chat apps to slip past platform monitoring.**
Once contact is made, scammers invent reasons to move you off WeChat or QQ and onto an "encrypted chat app" with a tiny user base — beyond the reach of mainstream platforms' anti-fraud monitoring, where the deep brainwashing happens in the dark. Anyone who tells you "let's switch apps to talk" is, 99% of the time, walking you somewhere with no oversight.

- 3️⃣ **Fraud apps take remote control of your phone.**
High-quality counterfeits of banking, insurance-claims, and investment apps come with built-in screen sharing, call forwarding, even remote control. Once installed, your card numbers, passwords, and verification codes are being stolen in real time without your knowledge. Remember: install apps only from official app stores; anything sent as an external link gets installed never.

- 4️⃣ **No more transfers — they want physical goods: cash, gold, phones, watches.**
As financial controls tighten and online transfers are increasingly intercepted and traced, scammers now have you withdraw cash or buy gold and phones, then send them by ride-hailing car, van courier (Huolala, China's "Uber for moving"), or errand runner to a designated spot — or send a "mule" to your door to collect. The money never touches the banking system, and recovery becomes far harder.

> ❌ "Anyone who tells you to withdraw cash or buy gold and then send it off by ride-hailing car is running a scam" — police in many regions have written this line into their standard rhymes, because it is the newest closing move of the harvest.

> ⚠️ Of the ten high-frequency types, you only need three sentences: a side gig that makes you pay upfront is a scam; an investment that "steadily earns" is a scam; a refund or claim that contacts you first is a scam.

> 🛡️ Make the "six nevers" a family rule: don't answer suspicious calls from strangers; don't click unknown links; don't install unknown apps; don't turn on screen sharing; don't transfer money to strangers; don't hand cash to a stranger who comes to collect "goods."

> ⚖️ "Mules" who withdraw cash or ferry gold for scammers are punished for concealing or disguising the proceeds of crime; knowingly transporting fraud money is a criminal offense. Don't be the final step of someone else's "a few hundred yuan a day" job.

> 🛑 Remember: always answer when 96110 (China's national anti-fraud hotline) calls — it is a warning intervention, meaning you or a family member is being worked by scammers right now. Having read this, forward it first to the person at home who is most confident "I could never be scammed."

## #165 · Exposed: The "Scan to Get a Free Skin" Scam — and the Gamers Whose Accounts Are Already Dead

`Black & gray industry` `💸 Money loss` `🚫 Account bans`

👉 A deep breakdown of the "livestream funnel / QR-code login hijack" account-theft playbook — so you don't get blinded by the bait of "free limited-edition skins" and end up with years of progress stolen and a permanent ban!

A black-industry chain targeting gamers is quietly spreading. Scam teams use "follow the stream and scan the code for a free limited skin / premium item" as bait, trick players into scanning, and steal their account assets in the process.

More alarming: many ordinary people recruited to host the streams think they're earning pocket-money commissions — not knowing they have long since become accomplices in account theft!

Today we take apart, once and for all, the 4 black-industry steps behind "scan for a free skin" — and the legal consequences. 💸

**🕵️‍♂️ Deep Breakdown: The 4 Steps of the "Scan-and-Steal" Harvest**

- 1️⃣ **Recruiting "streaming tools" on the cheap 📈**
Fraudsters recruit ordinary people online and talk them into registering on short-video platforms under their real identities and completing real-name verification (China's ID-linked account registration). The gang has them open livestreams and pays them commissions — turning them into the gang's on-the-ground funneling tools.

- 2️⃣ **Fake freebies; scan the code; grab the token 🪤**
Inside the livestream, scammers push viewers to scan a code for a "free premium skin." The moment a player scans it, the operator backstage uses packet-capture tools to intercept the player's login credentials (token). With that string of data they can log in remotely and seize the account — no password, no second verification needed!

- 3️⃣ **Sorted, sold, run into the ground 💰**
Once an account is stolen, the gang grades it by level, how much its owner had paid into the game, and skin value, then lists it in bulk on gray-market "card shops" and similar platforms. Most buyers use these accounts purely to cheat — the accounts are quickly detected by the game's operator and permanently banned!

- 4️⃣ **Harming others and themselves: a double loss ⚠️**
The people who hosted streams for strangers for pocket change are accomplices of an account-theft ring; the victim players lose not only the items and skins they paid real money for — years of game history, wiped out!

> 🛡️ **Protection guide and security line**

- 1️⃣ **Never stream for a stranger:** Don't chase small money. Never use your real-name account to livestream for someone else or rent out your streaming privileges.
- 2️⃣ **Never trust "free skins":** Every official in-game giveaway runs through an official announcement and an in-game channel. Don't trust a livestream's so-called "free skin" offer!
- 3️⃣ **Never scan unknown QR codes:** Don't casually scan web pages or QR codes of unknown origin. Treat any prompt saying "scan to authorize login" with maximum suspicion! 💡

Scams are everywhere, and scammers never run out. Stay alert; hold the line!

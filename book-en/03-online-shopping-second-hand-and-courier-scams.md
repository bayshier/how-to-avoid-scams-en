## #13 · Xianyu sellers, beware: the newest scam can hijack your parcel's delivery address mid-transit — you lose both the goods and the money

`Online Shopping, Second-hand & Courier` `Black-Industry Exposé` `💸 Money Lost`

A brand-new scam targeting Xianyu (China's largest second-hand marketplace) sellers has quietly gone live. The technique is jaw-dropping: the scammers can modify your parcel's delivery address while it is in transit! 😨

**📦 The real case**

A Xianyu seller listed a phone and agreed with the buyer to use the "Yanhuobao" inspection process (the platform's official escrow service: goods ship to a platform inspection center, which verifies the item before forwarding it to the buyer). No one expected that while the phone was en route to the inspection center, the courier's delivery address was tampered with by the scammer!

The situation now: Yanhuobao never receives the parcel, while the scammer brazenly files for a refund on Xianyu. The seller faces losing the phone to interception and never getting paid. 💸

**🧩 The cast of the scam, revealed**

- The Xianyu seller: the lamb awaiting slaughter.
- The proxy buyer: the scammer never shows their face — they pay someone else to place the order.
- The courier runner: a hired tool-person who collects the parcel.
- The scammer (the hand behind the curtain): the core that controls the entire play.

**🔑 The key question: how can the scammer change the address at all?**

This is the most terrifying part of the whole scheme. Once the seller uploads the tracking number on Xianyu, the scammer — using that tracking number plus the seller's phone number — can change the parcel's destination! The phone meant for the inspection center is hijacked mid-route. Is this a hole in the courier's system, or did the scammer obtain the seller's account details through illegal channels such as "social-worker databases" (black-market compilations of stolen personal data)?

**😱 The even nastier follow-up**

When the seller senses something is wrong and refuses the refund, the scammer escalates: breaking into the seller's other accounts (JD, SF Express), redeeming stolen points for goods, and performing all manner of malicious operations inside those apps — applying pressure to force the seller to refund and make the problem go away.

**🕵️ The playbook, summarized**

Scammers bulk-scan high-value listings on second-hand platforms ➡️ obtain seller information through illicit channels ➡️ hire a proxy buyer to purchase, agreeing to the inspection-escrow flow ➡️ use the harvested information to change the courier's delivery address and intercept the parcel mid-route ➡️ file for a refund-only on the grounds that "the inspection center never received the goods" ➡️ if the seller refuses, maliciously break into the seller's other app accounts to force compliance.

> 🛑 Warning: scams like this are cast wide. Second-hand sellers shipping high-value goods must raise their guard. We also call on the platforms to treat this seriously, fix the rules, and plug the loopholes.

## #104 · "My ex gave it to me, selling cheap": how second-hand platforms get played with genuine authentication tags — a fraud worth hundreds of thousands

`Online Shopping, Second-hand & Courier`

**"Hurt by a scumbag ex — the genuine sneakers / designer bag he gave me are of no use now. Selling below market, need the cash!"**

On Xiaohongshu and Xianyu, have you seen this viral copy: "dumped by my scumbag ex, transferring luxury goods cheap"? Don't assume you snagged a bargain — the "treasure you found" is a black-industry trap of "fake goods wearing real tags," designed to fleece you 💸!

**🔍 The full teardown: mixed real-and-fake stock + harvested authentication tags = hundreds of thousands in profit on fakes**

Step 1: Buy genuine items and "harvest" official authentication tags through the platform's own verification 🏷️
The crew first buys a genuine item for ¥2,800, then lists it on the second-hand platform with the "inspection guarantee" service and purchases it from themselves using a sock-puppet account. After passing the platform's official authentication, they lawfully obtain the genuine-item authentication tag and anti-counterfeit lock.

Step 2: Build the "dumped by my ex" persona, win sympathy, set the high-price trap 💔
Photograph the verified genuine item and post it on social platforms with copy like: "a gift from my scumbag ex — it upsets me just to look at it. Selling a few hundred below market, urgent," to lure victims in.

Step 3: Ship an ¥80 counterfeit wearing the genuine tag — pure arbitrage 📦
The moment the victim pays ¥2,000 for the order, the crew ships an ¥80-cost knockoff wearing the freshly harvested genuine tag! Seeing the official authenticity tag, the victim believes they've found a deal and accepts the parcel — and the crew nets nearly ¥2,000 instantly.

Step 4: Blame-shifting scripts to block complaints; one tag, many sales 🔄
What if a sharp buyer gets a second appraisal and discovers it's fake? The scammers have a fixed script ready: "I'm so sorry, sis — it was a gift from my ex; I never thought he was that kind of person!" They cheerfully take the return, then block the buyer. The genuine tag stays in circulation, hunting the next unsuspecting victim!

**🚨 Anti-trap reminder**

This "real tag, fake goods" play has already been copied across designer bags, wireless earbuds, limited-edition figures, high-end perfume, and more, across the entire second-hand market!

- Never trust sob-story personas: how many heartbroken women dumping luxury goods cheap can there be online? Every seductive persona exists for one purpose — to lower your guard!
- Bargain-hunting costs you big: trade through the platform's escrow and get an independent third-party authentication. Never transfer privately, and never blindly trust a hanging tag 🛡️!

The web is carpeted with tricks, and scammers never run out. Follow this channel — we've got your back.
